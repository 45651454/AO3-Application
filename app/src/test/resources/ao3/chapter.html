Stolen Time

outrageousboulefart

Chapter 11

Chapter Text

The soft rustle of clothing and the quiet click of a briefcase latch snapping shut broke the heavy silence of the bedroom. You stood by the vanity, smoothing down your blouse and slipping your earrings into place as you prepared for the long day ahead.

"I’m heading to work with Kai and Sora," you said evenly, your reflection catching the stiff, expectant set of your shoulders in the glass.

Today was the twins’ birthday—the day they turned five—yet you hadn't dropped a single hint, testing a quiet, heavy theory in the back of your mind.

Hiromi paused, a stack of files halfway inside his leather case. He looked up, entirely oblivious to the date, his mind still tethered to briefs and court dockets. 

"Oh, okay, honey. Don’t forget to eat and rest while you can." He closed the briefcase and straightened up, his dark eyes softening as he stepped closer to you and kissed your temple. "Do you have a shoot today?"

You nodded, keeping your expression carefully neutral. "I have filming and a photoshoot today."

A tense beat of silence stretched between you. You bit your lower lip, the quiet dread in your chest giving way to a cautious, painful curiosity as you turned to face him.

"Do you… know what day it is today?" you asked softly.

Hiromi blinked, a flicker of genuine confusion crossing his features. He searched his mind, scanning back through the endless blur of work schedules, meetings, and unread emails, completely unaware of the ticking clock.

"Um… Wednesday?" he said, his voice dropping slightly as he searched your face, unaware of the trapdoor he had just stepped into.

You swallowed the sharp, hot wave of disappointment pressing against the back of your throat. 

Of course he didn't remember. 

How could he, when he had spent the last three years treating the calendar like an enemy and leaving every single milestone, bedtime story, and doctor's appointment entirely on your shoulders? 

Even with his sudden attempts to turn things around these few weeks, the muscle memory of his neglect remained completely intact.

You forced a thin, fragile smile to your lips, nodding once as you turned back to grab your bag.

"Right. Have a good day at the office, Hiromi," you murmured, keeping your tone carefully empty to shield your heart from breaking any further.

Without waiting for his response, you walked out of the bedroom and down the stairs, leaving Hiromi standing alone in the quiet quietness of the master suite, the word Wednesday still hanging heavily in the empty air like a final, damning verdict.

You walked down the living room stairs, the heavy weight of Hiromi's oblivious answer still lingering in your chest. 

Before you could even reach the bottom landing, two little figures came tearing out from the hallway, their faces lit up with radiant, absolute excitement.

"Mommy! Mommy!" your boys chimed in unison, skidding to a halt right at your feet.

You knelt down instantly, letting the disappointment of a moment ago melt away as you looked into their bright, eager faces.

"Oh, my birthday boys are looking so handsome," you cooed, your voice thick with genuine adoration as you straightened Sora's little collar and patted Kai's soft hair.

Kai puffed out his chest, his eyes wide with pride. "I'm five now, Mommy!"

"Me too," Sora chimed in right after him, holding up a little hand with all five fingers splayed out proudly for you to inspect.

Hiromi walked down the grand staircase just as the boys were celebrating, a soft, hopeful smile gracing his features as he took in the scene of his family gathered in the living room.

"Hello, boys," he greeted warmly, his deep voice carrying a gentle afternoon cadence. "Both of you following Mommy to work?"

Sora nodded quietly, offering a small, polite smile that was a pale reflection of his usual brightness. Kai, on the other hand, stood completely still, his small frame rigid with anticipation as he stared up at his father, waiting for the one thing that mattered most today.

Hiromi paused, instantly sensing the heavy, waiting silence radiating from his oldest son. 

But trapped behind the blind spot of his own forgetfulness, he completely misinterpreted the boy's expectant gaze. He simply stepped forward, reached out, and ruffled Kai's hair with a casual affection.

"Behave for Mommy, okay?" Hiromi said, entirely unaware of the exact milestone ticking away right in front of him.

Kai’s shoulders slumped instantly, his lower lip trembling as the last ounce of childish hope vanished from his wide eyes. He turned his face away, burying his disappointment into the folds of your skirt.

You felt your heart fracture all over again for your little boy, the sharp sting of resentment flaring hot in your chest. You looked up at your husband, your gaze hardening with a cold, quiet disappointment as you pulled Kai protectively against your side.

"We’ll get going now," you said flatly, your voice entirely devoid of warmth.

Before Hiromi got a chance to speak or wonder at the sudden shift in the room's temperature, you turned on your heel and guided your boys toward the front door, leaving a clueless father standing alone in the quiet foyer.

You buckled them securely into their car seats and drove toward the agency’s headquarters, your hands gripping the steering wheel a little too tightly as the frustration burned in your chest. 

How could a father completely forget his only two sons' birthday? Even with his sudden attempts to turn things around for the past few weeks, missing a milestone this monumental proved just how far he still had to go to truly be present in their lives.

The heavy automatic doors of the agency slid open, and the suffocating weight of your annoyance vanished instantly, replaced by sheer astonishment.

Every single person in the massive lobby—from the top executives and the junior project coordinators to the security guards standing by the turnstiles—was wearing a colorful paper birthday hat. 

Hanging in bold, cheerful letters right above the main reception desk was a massive banner that read: HAPPY 5th BIRTHDAY, KAI AND SORA!

"Surprise!" a chorus of warm voices rang out across the floor.

Kai and Sora gasped in unison, their eyes going wide as saucers as they clutched the hems of your trousers, suddenly shy under the sudden wave of attention.

The people at your agency were a second family. They had watched your boys grow up from tiny, crying infants swaddled in blankets brought along to grueling photo shoots into bright, five-year-old distinguished boys who knew every corner of the building.

The head receptionist stepped out from behind her desk, holding out two oversized gift bags wrapped in shiny blue paper. 

"Happy birthday to our favorite little gentlemen!" she cooed, kneeling down to hand them their treats.

As the boys eagerly dove into the presents with beaming smiles, the warmth of the room wrapped around you, easing the bitter ache in your chest. 

At least here, in the world you had built alongside your career, your boys were celebrated, cherished, and never, ever forgotten.

Kaia, your fiercely no-nonsense vice president—a woman whose wardrobe usually consisted strictly of monochrome power suits and whose facial expression rarely deviated from chronic skepticism—stepped out from the crowd wearing a violently bright neon-pink party hat perched precariously on her neat bun.

She offered a slow, utterly unenthusiastic clap.

"Yay. Happy birthday, brats," Kaia drawled dryly, her tone dripping with mock enthusiasm as she looked down at the wide-eyed twins.

You choked back a sudden, undignified cough of laughter, covering your mouth with a hand as the sheer absurdity of seeing Kaia in neon pink registered in your brain.

Kaia snapped her gaze toward you, narrowing her sharp eyes into a dangerous glare.

"Don't get used to this, bossman," she warned, pointing a manicured finger your way. "I only agreed to wear the glowing accessory because the staffs threatened to tell everyone about my cookie addiction. My professional dignity is currently hanging by a very thin, fluorescent thread."

Kai and Sora giggled behind your legs, clutching their new gift bags that Kaia had given them as she finally cracked a reluctant, fond smile, bending down to ruffle Kai's hair.

Surrounded by the chaotic, genuine love of the people who had watched your family weather the hardest years, the lingering sting of the morning began to fade. 

“Where are my big birthday boys?!" a booming, impossibly theatrical voice echoed through the soaring marble lobby.

Before anyone could even blink, Satoru marched straight through the sliding glass doors—not quietly, but at the absolute head of an actual, live marching band.

A booming snare drum rattled the glass panels while a trumpet player blasted a triumphant, jazzy fanfare that made the security guards instantly jump into action before recognizing the chaotic white-haired menace leading the parade.

Satoru strutted forward wearing a tailored designer suit completely mismatched with an oversized, blinking LED birthday top hat and a neon green sash that read WORLD'S BEST PAPA TORU. 

He swept off his sunglasses, his striking blue eyes flashing with pure, unadulterated excitement as he scanned the room.

The lobby erupted into absolute pandemonium. Kai and Sora shrieked with unadulterated joy, dropping their gift bags and bolting across the polished floor straight toward him.

"Papa Toru!" they yelled in unison.

Satoru dropped to his knees with zero regard for his expensive tailoring, scooping both boys up into a massive, laughing double-hug as they collided into his chest. 

He spun them around in a wide circle, the brass instruments blaring another cheerful note as Kaia pinched the bridge of her nose in utter, long-suffering disbelief.

"You absolute lunatic," Kaia muttered under her breath, though she was fighting back a grin as the entire agency cheered.

Satoru peppered both of the boys' faces with loud, exaggerated kisses, completely unfazed by the spectacle he had just caused in the middle of a high-end corporate headquarters. 

He looked up over their heads, his gaze finding yours across the room, softening with a warm, steady affection that felt entirely effortless.

"Did you really think I'd let my favorite little guys turn five without a proper parade?" Satoru grinned, setting the giggling boys back down on their feet while keeping a hand proudly on each of their shoulders.

"Of course I would never forget to celebrate the birth giver of two amazing boys, even if it's not her birthday yet," Satoru teased smoothly, flashing a dazzling, effortless grin as he straightened up from the boys.

With a theatrical flourish, he reached into his jacket pocket and pulled out a small, polished silver locket on a delicate chain. He flicked it open with one finger, revealing a miniature, beautifully framed photograph of you laughing candidly while holding Kai and Sora close on a sunlit afternoon.

Kaia rolled her eyes so hard it looked painful, crossing her arms over her monochrome power suit. 

"Don't call your boss that..." she muttered dryly, though there was an undeniable smirk playing at the corner of her lips.

Satoru just winked at her, completely unbothered, before stepping across the polished floor toward you. He gently held out the locket, his blue eyes softening as he caught your gaze.

"For you," Satoru said softly, the playful showmanship melting away into something steady and genuine. "Because you're the one who carried them, raised them, and kept this whole circus running while everyone else was looking the other way. You deserve to be celebrated today just as much as they do."

The sweet, thoughtful gesture hung in the warm air of the lobby, a stark and painful contrast to the cold, oblivious dismissal you had received from Hiromi just hours earlier. 

You reached out, your fingers brushing against the cool silver as Satoru carefully fastened the chain around your neck, letting the locket rest right against your collarbone.

Kai and Sora crowded around you, eagerly reaching up to inspect the tiny picture glowing against your blouse.

"Look, Mommy! It's us!" Sora cheered, clapping his hands.

"Now! Let's us sing for the best boys in the whole world," Satoru announced theatrically, throwing his arms wide open as the marching band smoothly transitioned from their triumphant fanfare into a bouncy, jazzy rhythm.

The entire lobby chimed in, clapping their hands in unison to the beat. 

Kaia reluctantly clapped along from the side, tapping her high heel in time with the music, while the receptionist held up a massive, multi-tiered cake covered in glowing candles that someone had managed to smuggle out of the back breakroom.

Kai and Sora clapped their hands excitedly, their earlier disappointment completely forgotten as they bounced on their heels right in front of the glittering confection.

"Happy birthday to you, happy birthday to you…" Satoru belted out in a surprisingly rich, dramatic baritone, taking the lead as the entire agency joined in a roaring chorus of voices.

You stood right beside your boys, the silver locket resting warm against your chest, and let the sheer, unfiltered joy of the moment wash over you. You knelt down between Kai and Sora as the song reached its climax.

"Make a big wish, my loves," you whispered, smiling proudly as they squeezed their eyes shut tight, taking a massive, coordinated breath.

With a joyful puff, they blew out every single candle in one go, sending a tiny cloud of smoke curling into the air as the lobby erupted into deafening cheers, applause, and confetti poppers going off overhead. 

Satoru scooped Sora up onto his shoulders while Kai grabbed your hand, grinning ear to ear as the celebration truly began.

"You remembered, Papa Toru," Sora said softly, his little voice laced with absolute, untainted adoration as he leaned his head heavily against Satoru’s broad chest, completely safe and entirely cherished.

Kai chimed in right after, his small hands gripping the edge of Satoru’s blazer. "Thank you for remembering, Papa."

Satoru’s theatrical facade fractured instantly, replaced by a raw, profound wave of tenderness. His chest tightened painfully at the simple, heartbreaking gratitude coming from children who had grown up expecting so little. 

He wrapped his arms securely around both of them, pulling them flush against him as if he could physically shield them from every cold, careless oversight of the world.

God, they are such angels, Satoru thought, a fierce, protective heat rushing through his veins.

He looked down at the bright, trusting eyes of the boys, and then his gaze drifted up to meet yours through the sea of confetti and smiling faces. In that quiet, shared look, a silent vow settled deep into his bones. 

Satoru swore right then and there that he would never, ever let anything or anyone ever hurt them—or you—again. You had carried the crushing weight of this family entirely on your own for far too long, and he would burn the world down before he let that kind of loneliness touch your door ever again.

"Of course I remember. I love you both so much," Satoru said, his voice thick with unbridled emotion as he showered them with loud, affectionate kisses against their cheeks and hair, making the boys erupt into fits of helpless, giggly laughter.

"We love you too!" they chorused back, throwing their arms around his neck.

The warmth of the moment wrapped around you like a protective shield, washing away the bitter taste of the morning. Satoru didn't just show up for the milestones. 

He made sure your boys felt like the most important creatures on earth, anchoring them with a steady, fierce devotion that you hadn't realized you were starving for until he walked through those doors.

As the agency team swarmed forward with plates of cake and juice boxes for the twins, Satoru gently set the boys down as he stepped closer to you. The noisy, joyful chaos of confetti and chatter faded into a distant hum.

"You okay, Mama?" Satoru murmured quietly, his striking blue eyes searching your face with a tender, perceptive intensity that missed nothing. He reached out, his thumb lightly brushing away a stray speck of glitter from your cheek, his touch feather-light and entirely grounding.

You smiled softly, the tension finally leaving your shoulders as you reached out to squeeze his hand. "Thank you, Satoru. I know this was your plan."

Satoru let out a light, melodramatic scoff, placing a hand over his heart in mock offense. "Mine and Kaia’s. She’ll kill me and feed me to the office shredder if she knows I tried to take all the credit."

Across the lobby, Kaia paused mid-bite of a chocolate cupcake, shooting Satoru a lethal, narrowing glare from across the room that confirmed every word he just said. 

Satoru just grinned back, entirely unrepentant, before turning his attention back to you with a warm, steady gaze that made the lingering chill of the morning completely disappear.

"Oh yeah. Where’s your husband?" Satoru asked, his tone casual, though his sharp blue eyes immediately caught the sudden, subtle shift in your posture.

You slowly dropped Satoru’s hand, your fingers sliding away from his warm palm as you looked past him toward your twins. 

Kai and Sora were sitting cross-legged on the polished lobby floor, completely engrossed in animated conversation with the agency staff while trading colorful frosting art from their multi-tiered birthday cake.

"He's at work..." you said softly, your voice barely carrying over the cheerful din of the party.

Satoru didn't need any further explanation. The heavy pause, the defensive drop of your shoulders, the quiet, resigned shadow that crossed your face—it all painted a crystal-clear picture. 

Satoru knew what that meant.

Your husband forgot. 

A quiet, hard glint flickered behind Satoru’s striking eyes, the playful, theatrical showmanship instantly stripping away to reveal a fierce, protective edge. 

He didn't curse or throw out empty insults, but his jaw tightened perceptibly as he looked from you to the innocent boys laughing on the floor. 

To miss a five-year milestone—to be entirely absent while another man stepped in to watch your children light up with unbridled joy—was a brand of negligence that Satoru found utterly unforgivable.

He took a half-step closer, his shoulder shielding you slightly from the bustling crowd of coworkers. 

"The absolute fool," Satoru muttered under his breath, his voice low, steady, and dripping with quiet indignation. "You and those boys carry the weight of an entire world, and he can't even look at a calendar. I swear, if he ever figures out what he’s losing, it’ll be lightyears too late."

You let out a shaky, quiet breath, the validation of your anger acting as both a balm and a sting. You didn't defend Hiromi. You couldn't. Not anymore. The words just felt heavy.

For years, the excuses you had manufactured for him had worn paper-thin, fraying until nothing was left but the stark, exhausting reality of parenting alone within a marriage.

Across the room, Kaia caught the shift in the air. She set her cupcake down on a nearby side table, her sharp eyes narrowing as she observed the two of you. 

With her neon pink party hat still perched crookedly on her bun, she navigated through the sea of celebrating employees with purposeful strides, closing the distance until she stood right beside Satoru.

"Everything okay over here? Do I need to start clearing my schedule to help bury someone?" Kaia asked dryly, her tone deadpan, though her gaze flicked knowingly toward you with fierce, unwavering solidarity.

"Just marveling at the sheer, Olympic-level audacity of absent fathers," Satoru replied, his voice laced with dry sarcasm as he kept a protective, anchoring presence close to your side.

You looked down at the silver locket resting against your collarbone, feeling the cool metal warm against your skin. The contrast between the home you lived in and the life you stood in right now felt vast and unbridgeable. 

But as Kai looked up from the floor, flashing a bright, gap-toothed grin as he held up a piece of candy for you to see, the constriction in your chest loosened. 

You smiled back, waving him on, knowing that no matter how many dates a oblivious husband missed, your boys would never have to question who truly showed up for them.

Kaia scoffed, crossing her arms over her blazer with a look of absolute, unvarnished exasperation. "Well, isn’t that just normal for him. I don’t understand why you still gave that man a chance. Just marry Gojo here. He’s handsome, heir to a corporate titan, and devotedly in love with you."

On cue, before either of you could brace for it, she casually reached out and shoved Satoru forward right into your personal space.

Satoru stumbled half a step, completely caught off guard by the sudden betrayal. The cool, collected, theatrical facade shattered in a fraction of a second, and a deep, mortified flush raced straight up his neck, turning his striking face an unmistakable, brilliant red.

"Ay, yo…" Satoru stammered, his usual smooth delivery completely abandoning him as he caught his balance, awkwardly hovering inches away from you. 

He shot a lethal, wide-eyed glare over his shoulder at Kaia, who just stared back with a completely deadpan, innocent expression, sipping her juice box like she hadn't just detonated a social grenade in the middle of the lobby.

Kaia doubled down without missing a beat, leaning against a marble pillar with all the nonchalance of someone discussing the weather.

"At least Gojo doesn’t forget your sons’ birthday," Kaia said, her voice cutting clearly through the ambient party noise. "He celebrated their birthday five times now. FIVE. That’s how old the twins are, mind you."

Satoru rubbed the back of his neck, the bright blush still burning hot across his cheekbones as he tried to look anywhere else except directly at you, though his blue eyes kept darting back with a nervous, hopeful intensity.

"Okay, first of all, you're making me sound like a stalker keeping tally marks in a dark room," Satoru muttered defensively, though his lips twitched into an involuntary, crooked smile. 

He cleared his throat and looked down at you, his voice softening. "Second of all... of course. I remembered. It’s not that hard."

You stood frozen between them, your breath catching in your throat as the sheer weight of Kaia's words sank into your chest. 

Five times. 

Satoru had marked every single year of your boys' lives, standing quietly by your side through the sleepless nights during late night shoots, the toddler meltdowns, and the quiet milestones when Hiromi had been miles away behind a stack of legal briefs or locked in his office.

The warmth of the silver locket against your collarbone suddenly felt overwhelming—not because it was a flashy gift, but because it carried the history of a man who chose to stay, who chose to remember, and who loved your boys as fiercely as you did.

You looked up at Satoru, the familiar flutter of your heart warring with the deep, aching gratitude in your chest. 

You didn't know what to say to a man who had memorized your life so completely, but as Satoru offered a soft, slightly shy smile that completely melted his usual untouchable bravado, you felt somewhat grateful.

You shook your head slowly, a faint, tired sigh slipping past your lips as you pulled your gaze away from Satoru's intense blue eyes.

"I'm married," you reminded them quietly, the reminder tasting like ash in your mouth.

Before the words could even fully settle in the air, Kaia loudly slammed her hands over her ears, screwed her eyes shut, and started humming a loud, obnoxious tune.

"La la la! Whatever! I can't hear you!" Kaia sang out deliberately, completely unfazed by your attempt to inject a shred of professional or marital boundary into the conversation. 

She lowered her hands just enough to shoot a pointed look between the two of you. "Technicalities. Minor details. Details that clearly escape your legally registered roommate who can't even tell you what day of the week his own kids were popped into the world."

Satoru let out a breath that sounded half-laugh, half-groan, running a hand through his messy white hair as the bright flush slowly receded from his face, leaving behind a softer, more complicated expression. 

Yet Satoru didn't push. He never pushed, always giving you the space to draw whatever lines you needed, even when those lines kept you tied to a man who didn't deserve half the loyalty you gave him.

"Well… She’s not wrong, you know," Satoru murmured gently, his voice dropping low enough that only you could hear over the distant chatter of the marching band and the boys. 

He stepped just a fraction closer, his gaze steady, warm, and entirely unmasked. "But you don't have to entertain her matchmaking schemes."

Across the room, Sora suddenly shrieked with laughter as one of the junior designers balanced a paper plate on his head, completely breaking the heavy, electric tension hanging between the three of you.

You looked back down at the silver locket resting against your chest, letting the cool metal ground you. You didn't have an answer for Kaia, and you certainly didn't have an answer for the messy, fracturing reality waiting for you back at home.
